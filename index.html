Engineering in Practice

Featured R&D

Working builds on a single Renesas RA8P1, measured on the board, not in slides.

Renesas EK-RA8P1 Ethos-U55 NPU On-Device Vision

Real-Time Pose Estimation

Live body tracking on one microcontroller: camera to 17-point skeleton entirely on the device, with no PC, cloud or network. Moving inference to the Ethos-U55 NPU cut the AI step from 155 ms to 12 ms, so the skeleton updates on every camera frame with about 70% of frame time to spare. Images never leave the device; only body points do.

Result: 12 ms inference, 12.9× faster than CPU-only
Toward: Presence and fall-detection research

Renesas EK-RA8P1 µT-Kernel 3.0 Vision + Language

Conversational Hand Gestures

Two AI workloads on one chip: hand-gesture recognition on the Ethos-U55 NPU and an 8-bit language model on the Cortex-M85, scheduled with fixed µT-Kernel priorities so the camera never drops a frame. A child converses through hand shapes alone, and no camera data leaves the device.

Result: 2.61 tok/s alongside live video
Memory: 1.2 MB of 1.9 MB SRAM used

Our Engineering Philosophy

Certainty on Real Hardware

A device feature is only done when it is proven on the chip, verified on your own board, and kept accurate after launch. That is the work we do, at a fixed scope and a fixed price, never by the hour.

How We Work

Three fixed-scope steps. Start with the first; continue only if it pays off.

STEP 01
Feasibility Study

2–6 weeks, fixed price. Can your model run on the chosen chip at the accuracy, latency and memory you need? You get a measured answer and a build plan.

STEP 02
Feature Project

Fixed scope. The feature integrated, optimized and verified on your board, with a hardware-in-the-loop regression rig.

STEP 03
Support Retainer

Annual. Sustaining engineering and model care that keep the feature accurate in the field.
