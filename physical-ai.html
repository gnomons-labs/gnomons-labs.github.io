What we mean

Physical AI is AI that senses people and acts in the real world

A robot, a kiosk, a care-room sensor or a fitness mirror all have the same first job: work out where people are and what they are doing, then act on it within a fraction of a second. That first step is the part we build.

Our part is the layer at the sensor. Camera and sensor input in, a skeleton, a gesture or a presence signal out, and a decision made on the same chip — inside the timing budget of an embedded system.

Images never need to leave the device, so there is no cloud latency, no network dependency and a simpler privacy story.

How we contribute

From raw sensor data to a decision on the device

Four layers, each sized to fit on a microcontroller alongside the rest of the product.

01 · Sense

Get clean input from the sensors

Camera capture over MIPI CSI-2 or parallel interfaces, with other sensors joined over SPI, I2C and UART and combined at the decision level.

Camera 60 GHz radar Thermal / ToF IMU
02 · Understand

Turn the picture into a few useful points

A 17-point body skeleton or 21 hand joints per frame, then simple rules on top that turn those points into a gesture, a posture or a presence signal.

Body pose Hand gestures Presence / fall Bed-exit
03 · Respond

Decide and act on the same chip

Local decisions with no network round-trip. Vision and a small language model can share one MCU, so a device can take turns with a person through gestures alone.

Vision + language model Gesture turn-taking
04 · Stay real-time

Never delay the camera or the display

Fixed-priority scheduling keeps capture and display on time; the AI runs in the spare CPU time and on the NPU.

Architecture

Sense, understand and respond on one microcontroller

The camera path and the AI share one chip. The NPU reads the body or the hands; the CPU turns that into a decision; the RTOS keeps the camera and display on schedule throughout.

Proof on silicon

Measured on the EK-RA8P1, not estimated

Two working builds on a single Renesas RA8P1 with its Ethos-U55 NPU. Every number below was measured on the board.

12.9×
faster body tracking with the NPU than on the CPU alone

Pose inference 12 ms on the NPU vs. 155 ms CPU-only · two NPU models together use 1.2 MB of 1.9 MB on-chip SRAM · no cloud dependencies: images never leave the device.
